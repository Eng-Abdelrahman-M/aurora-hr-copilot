# Code of Conduct — Aurora Dynamics

Document ID: POL-COC-008 · Owner: People Operations & Legal · Last revised: 2026-01-10

## 1. Expected behavior

Treat colleagues, customers, and partners with respect. Harassment,
discrimination, bullying, and retaliation are prohibited in all work
contexts, including offsites, chat, and video calls.

## 2. Harassment and discrimination

- Prohibited grounds include race, color, religion, sex, gender identity or
  expression, sexual orientation, national origin, age, disability, and any
  other legally protected characteristic.
- Sexual harassment — unwelcome advances, requests for sexual favors, or
  conduct creating a hostile environment — is grounds for discipline up to
  and including termination.

## 3. Reporting concerns

- Report concerns to your manager, any People Operations partner, or through
  the **anonymous ethics line** at ethics.aurora.example.
- Reports of harassment or discrimination are **escalated to a trained HR
  case manager within one business day**. Do not investigate on your own.
- **Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith is itself
  a violation** of this Code and is treated with the same severity as the
  underlying conduct.

## 4. Conflicts of interest

Disclose outside employment, financial interests in vendors or competitors,
and personal relationships that intersect with reporting lines to People
Operations. Managers may not directly supervise a family member or partner.

## 5. Confidentiality

Employees must protect confidential business and personnel information both
during and after employment, consistent with the Data Security Policy
(POL-SEC-005).

## 6. Sensitive matters and escalation

Some matters — threats of violence, self-harm, legal claims, regulatory
complaints — must always be escalated to People Operations immediately and
are outside the scope of self-service guidance. When in doubt, escalate; an
unnecessary escalation is never penalized.

## 7. Respectful communication

Aurora Dynamics is a written-first company, and most disagreements happen in
text where tone is easily misread. Employees are expected to assume good
faith, criticise the work rather than the person, and take heated exchanges
into a call rather than escalating them in a channel.

Public channels are the default; private messages are for genuinely private
matters. Decisions taken in a private conversation that affect others are
summarised back into the relevant channel, so that colleagues in other time
zones are not disadvantaged [POL-RW-002 section 8].

Jokes, images and language that a reasonable colleague would find demeaning
are not acceptable in any channel, including informal ones, and including
where everyone present appears comfortable.

## 8. Substance use

Employees must not be impaired by alcohol or drugs while working, whether in
an office, at a customer site or at home. Alcohol at company events is
moderate and always accompanied by non-alcoholic options; nobody is pressured
to drink, and events are not scheduled in venues where attendance requires
drinking.

An employee whose use of alcohol or drugs is affecting their work is
encouraged to approach People Operations or the employee assistance programme
[POL-BEN-009 section 9]. Seeking help voluntarily is treated as a health
matter and is confidential; it is not a disciplinary matter.

## 9. Relationships at work

Personal relationships between colleagues are permitted and are not the
company's business, with one exception: a relationship within a reporting line
must be disclosed to People Operations, who will arrange for the reporting
line to change. The disclosure is confidential and does not affect either
person's standing.

Neither party in such a relationship may take part in decisions about the
other's pay, promotion, performance review or hiring.

## 10. Investigations

Where a concern is raised under section 3, People Operations acknowledges it
within two business days and explains what will happen next. Investigations
are conducted by someone with no personal involvement in the matter, and both
the person raising the concern and the person it concerns are heard.

Information is shared only with those who need it to investigate or decide.
Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, or who takes
part in an investigation, is itself a serious disciplinary matter — regardless
of whether the original concern is upheld.

Where an investigation finds misconduct, outcomes range from a written
expectation of change through to dismissal, proportionate to the conduct.
The person who raised the concern is told that the matter has concluded and,
where it affects them directly, what has changed.

## 11. Speaking publicly about the company

Employees may say they work at Aurora Dynamics and describe their role.
Product roadmaps, customer names, financial figures and internal metrics are
confidential [POL-SEC-005 section 5] and are not discussed publicly, including
in conference talks, blog posts and social media, without approval from
Communications.

Employees speaking publicly on their own behalf about matters unrelated to the
company do so as individuals, and should make that clear where confusion is
possible. Aurora Dynamics does not police personal opinions.
